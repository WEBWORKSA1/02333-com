Lucky Numbers in Chinese Business

If your customers include Chinese speakers in Beijing, Singapore, Vancouver or Sydney, numbers are part of your brand. Here's the practical playbook.

The core rules

DigitSounds likeBusiness signal
8发 fā, prosperStrongly positive. Prices, phone numbers, floor numbers.
6顺 / 溜, smoothPositive: “things go smoothly”.
9久 jiǔ, lastingPositive for relationships, weddings, loyalty.
4死 sǐ, deathAvoid, especially at the end of a number.
250二百五, foolNever use as a price or quantity.

1. Pricing

Retail prices ending in 8 (¥88, ¥168, ¥888) are extremely common in Chinese-speaking markets. They play the same role as the Western “.99” ending. Avoid endings in 4, and never price a bundle at 250. For gifts, round lucky amounts (¥520, ¥1,314, ¥888) carry an emotional message of their own.

2. Phone numbers & license plates

Numbers full of 8s trade at a premium, and memorable ones have sold at auction for very large sums. For a business line, avoid 4s and aim for an 8 or a 6 near the end. Run yours through the analyzer →

3. Addresses & floors

Many buildings in Chinese-speaking cities skip the 4th, 14th and 24th floors. Units with 4s can be harder to sell or rent to some buyers, while 8s can command a premium.

4. Launch & signing dates

The Beijing 2008 Olympics opened at 8:08 pm on 08/08/2008. Businesses often pick dates with 6, 8 or 9 for openings and contract signings, and May 20 (“520”) is now a huge romance-driven shopping day.

Pro tip: Culture varies. Cantonese speakers have their own sound-alikes (for example 3 ≈ 生 “life”), and many younger consumers treat all of this playfully. Understand the beliefs, but don't overdo it.
