What Does 02333 (and 2333) Mean?

Short answer: 2333 means “LOL” in Chinese internet slang, and 02333 is a playful “you — hahaha!”. Here's the longer story.

1. Where 233 came from

In the early 2000s, one of China's first big online communities, the forum MOP (猫扑), had a set of numbered emoticons. Emoticon #233 showed a face laughing so hard it was pounding the floor. Users started typing the code “233” instead of inserting the picture, and the number became shorthand for laughing out loud.

From there, 233 spread to video sites with scrolling “bullet comments” (弹幕 dànmù), where viewers type reactions that fly across the screen. On anime, gaming and comedy videos you'll often see hundreds of “2333333” racing past at once.

2. Why more 3s means more laughing

2333haha, LOL
23333hahaha, ROFL
2333333333I literally can't breathe

Like “hahahaha” in English, the length shows how hard you're laughing. Nobody counts the 3s. Just add more when it's funnier.

3. What the 0 in 02333 adds

In number slang, 0 is often read as 你 (nǐ, “you”). The best-known example is 520 = 我爱你 (“I love you”). Put a 0 in front of 2333 and you get “you — hahaha!”: a friendly, teasing way to say someone cracked you up.

Note: 02333 is not a fixed dictionary term the way 2333 is. It's a natural, playful combination that native speakers understand, and it's the reading this site is named after.

4. 02333 in finance

The same digits come up in a completely different place. Hong Kong stock codes are often written with a leading zero, so the listing with code 2333, the Hong Kong–listed H-shares of a major Chinese carmaker, is widely quoted as 02333. That's a coincidence of formatting, not slang. This site has no connection to that company or to any exchange, and nothing here is investment advice.

5. How to use it (without being cringe)

  • ✅ Reply to a friend's joke: “02333 你太逗了” (“You're hilarious, lol”).
  • ✅ Comment on a funny video: “2333333”.
  • ⚠️ In formal emails or business chats, write 哈哈 or skip it.
  • ❌ Don't use it about something sad. It reads as mocking.
